# AgentFence Research Sources

## NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Source: [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)

NIST describes AI RMF as a voluntary framework intended to help organizations incorporate trustworthiness considerations into AI products, services, and systems. Its stated core functions are Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. NIST released the Generative AI Profile in July 2024 to help organizations identify unique generative-AI risks and align risk-management actions with their goals and priorities.

## NIST AI 600-1, Generative AI Profile

Source: [NIST AI 600-1 PDF](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)

The profile describes lifecycle-oriented governance for generative AI and focuses on governance, content provenance, pre-deployment testing, and incident disclosure. It identifies risks that can be unique to or exacerbated by generative AI, including data privacy, information security, human-AI configuration and over-reliance, information integrity, and non-transparent component integration. The profile supports the AgentFence design position that control points, policy checks, approvals, audit evidence, data protections, and staged testing are necessary around consequential AI actions.

## Preparing for AI Agent Governance

Source: [Partnership on AI, Preparing for AI Agent Governance](https://partnershiponai.org/resource/preparing-for-ai-agent-governance/)

The September 2025 report states that the impacts of AI agents remain uncertain, while investment continues; it emphasizes evidence collection, sandboxes, testbeds, monitoring, and real-time failure detection as early governance priorities. This supports a cautious go-to-market claim: AgentFence should be positioned as an enterprise control layer that enables staged adoption and evidence, not as a guarantee that autonomous systems are risk-free.

## EU General-Purpose AI Model FAQ

Source: [European Commission, General-Purpose AI Models in the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/general-purpose-ai-models-ai-act-questions-answers)

The European Commission explains that downstream AI systems require technical documentation and value-chain understanding from general-purpose model providers. For systemic-risk models, the AI Act includes expectations around risk assessment and mitigation, serious-incident reporting, and cybersecurity. AgentFence is not itself a statement of regulatory compliance, but its policy, evidence, approval, and runtime-control functions can support organizations' system-level governance workflows.

## Generative AI Economic Potential

Source: [McKinsey, The Economic Potential of Generative AI](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-economic-potential-of-generative-ai-the-next-productivity-frontier)

McKinsey estimated that generative-AI use cases it analyzed could add the equivalent of $2.6 trillion to $4.4 trillion annually across 63 use cases. It identified customer operations, marketing and sales, software engineering, and research and development as accounting for approximately 75% of the potential annual value in that analysis. These are market-context estimates, not AgentFence revenue forecasts or a valuation claim. They support the product thesis that value-producing agentic workflows require proportional controls as they gain access to enterprise systems and data.
